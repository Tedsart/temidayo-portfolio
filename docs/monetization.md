# How to Make Money From This Website

Plain language, Nigeria-practical, in the order that actually earns.

The site you have is a **credibility machine**: it proves you can turn messy
data into decisions. Money comes when three things meet —

> **An offer** (what someone buys) + **a way to pay you** (rails) + **traffic**
> (people who see the offer).

You have the credibility. This document closes the other two.

## 2. Route A — Client services (start here)

### The offer: sell packages, not hours
Clients buy certainty. Three packages on your site beat "I charge per hour":

1. **Dashboard Sprint — 5–7 days.** One decision-ready dashboard from your
   data (Excel / Power BI / Looker Studio). Fixed price.
2. **Analysis & Report — 2–3 weeks.** Clean the data, analyse it, hand back a
   written report with findings and recommendations. Fixed price.
3. **Fractional Analyst — monthly retainer.** You are their analyst X days a
   month: dashboards refreshed, reports delivered, questions answered.
   **This is the one that pays your rent** — recurring, predictable income.

### Pricing guidance (starting ranges — adjust as you learn)
These are *starting points for a strong beginner-to-intermediate portfolio*,
not fixed rules. They differ by client geography; a UK startup and a Lagos SME
are different budgets.

| Offer | Local (NG) client | International client |
|---|---|---|
| Dashboard sprint | ₦150k – ₦400k | $300 – $900 |
| Analysis + report | ₦250k – ₦700k | $600 – $2,000 |
| Monthly retainer | ₦200k – ₦600k/mo | $500 – $1,800/mo |
| Hourly consulting | ₦15k – ₦40k/hr | $25 – $60/hr |

**How to raise your price honestly:** take your first 2–3 clients at the lower
end *in exchange for a testimonial and permission to publish the work*. Publish
each as a case study. After three published results, raise prices by ~25%.
Repeat. Your portfolio *is* your pricing power — that's why this site exists.

### Rules that protect you
- **50% upfront, 50% on delivery.** No exceptions, no "we'll pay after review".
- **One-page scope** before you start: what data, what question, what you
  deliver, how many revisions (2 is standard), what happens if the data is
  unusable.
- **No free full projects.** A free 30-minute call is generous enough.
- **Invoice in writing** (email or PDF), with your rate, deliverable and
  payment details.

## 3. Getting paid from Nigeria (the rails)

You need two things: a way for **Nigerians** to pay you, and a way for
**foreigners** to pay you.

### Foreign clients (USD/GBP/EUR)
- **Wise** or **Grey** (grey.co) or **Payoneer** — give you foreign account
  details (USD/GBP/EUR) so a client abroad pays you like a local bank
  transfer. This is the simplest, cheapest route for client work.
- **Domiciliary account** (GTB/Zenith/Access) — works, but slower and
  paperwork-heavy.
- **Upwork / Contra / Fiverr** — they handle contracts, escrow and payment
  (fee ~10–20%). Use them for *discovery* and safety, then move good clients
  to direct payment after the first project.
- **Lemon Squeezy** or **Paddle** — best for selling *digital products*
  abroad. They are "merchant of record": they handle VAT, invoices and tax
  compliance for you. Payout via Payoneer/PayPal/bank.

### Nigerian clients (NGN)
- **Paystack** or **Flutterwave** — instant NGN bank transfer / card
  payments; you can generate a **payment link** and send it in WhatsApp or
  paste it on your site. Perfect for invoices and local product sales.
- **Direct bank transfer** — fine for SMEs you already trust.

### Selling digital products
- **Selar** (Nigerian) or **Paystack Storefront** — easy NGN + card checkout.
- **Gumroad** — easiest for international audience; payouts via PayPal/bank.

### Your CV = your pricing proof
Your CV download on the site is already a lead magnet. Keep it current.

## 4. Route B — Digital products (month 2)

Low effort to maintain, sells while you sleep. Ideas that fit your brand:

1. **Dashboard templates** — Power BI / Looker Studio / Excel, themed for
   Nigerian SMEs: sales tracker, inventory, HR, NGO programme monitoring.
   Price ₦5k–₦25k (or $10–$40).
2. **"Data cleaning checklist" + Excel/Sheets toolkit** — cheap entry product
   (₦3k–₦10k) that builds your email list.
3. **E-book: "How to read a Nigerian inflation report"** — your existing
   inflation project is literally the sample chapter. ₦5k–₦15k.
4. **Cohort course: Data Storytelling in 4 weeks** — ₦50k–₦150k per person,
   10–20 people per cohort.

Sell via Selar/Gumroad, link from a **Products** section on the site, and
promote with a free version first (lead magnet → email list → paid product).

## 5. Route C — Teaching & workshops (month 3)

- **Corporate training**: "Excel/Power BI for your team" — sell to Nigerian
  SMEs, banks' SME desks, NGOs, and civil-service departments. One day of
  training: ₦300k–₦1.5m.
- **Public cohort / webinar**: ₦5k–₦30k per seat. Use your site as the
  registration page and your email list as the audience.
- **University / bootcamp guest sessions**: low pay, high credibility — and
  they send you clients.

## 6. What to change on the website (the money pages)

Your site is beautiful but currently has **no ask**. Add, in this order:

1. **"Work with me" page** (`/services`) — the 3 packages above, prices or
   "from ₦…", what's included, and a **Book a 20-minute call** button
   (Calendly or Tally). *This single page is the highest-value change
   available to you.*
2. **Booking + WhatsApp** — Nigerian clients convert on WhatsApp. Add a
   floating "Chat on WhatsApp" button next to the contact form.
3. **Testimonials section** — three short quotes with name, role, company.
   Social proof sells more than any copy you write.
4. **Products section** — link your Gumroad/Selar store.
5. **Lead magnet** — "Get my dashboard checklist" behind an email field
   (Mailchimp/Buttondown free tier) so you own an audience.
6. **Keep publishing case studies** — every published project is both SEO
   (Google finds you for "data analyst Lagos", "Power BI dashboard Nigeria")
   and sales collateral.
7. **Analytics (optional)** — V1 skipped it on purpose. When you start
   selling, add Plausible or GA4 so you know which pages produce enquiries.

All of this is CMS-editable or a small code addition — say the word and I'll
build the services page, booking link and payment buttons into the site.

## 7. Traffic: how people actually find you

Your site being on Google is a *slow* channel. These are faster:

1. **LinkedIn (your account is already linked in the footer)** — post once a
   week: a chart you made, "here's what most people miss in this dataset", a
   before/after of a messy spreadsheet. End with "portfolio in the comments".
   This is where analysts get hired.
2. **Direct outreach** — 5 personalised emails/DMs a day to Nigerian SMEs,
   NGOs, fintechs and agencies who clearly have messy data. Attach a relevant
   case study. 100 messages → 5 calls → 1 client is a normal ratio.
3. **Marketplaces** — Upwork, Contra, Fiverr, Toptal (later). Use while you
   build your own pipeline.
4. **Communities** — Nigerian tech/analytics WhatsApp & Slack groups, Data
   Science Nigeria, women-in-data groups. Helpful answers → DMs → clients.
5. **Google (long game)** — the sitemap and case-study pages you already have
   do this quietly in the background. Keep adding projects.
